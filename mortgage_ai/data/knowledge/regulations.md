# Mortgage Regulatory Compliance Guide

## TRID — TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure Rule

TRID (pronounced "trid") combines two federal disclosure requirements: the Truth in Lending Act (TILA) and the Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA). It was implemented by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) in 2015.

### Loan Estimate (LE)
**Required by:** 12 CFR § 1026.19(e)
**When:** Must be delivered within 3 business days of receiving a complete loan application.
**Purpose:** Shows the borrower estimated loan terms, projected monthly payments, and closing costs.

**Complete Application Triggers (Reg Z):** Receipt of all 6 pieces of information:
1. Borrower name
2. Monthly income
3. Social Security number (for credit check)
4. Property address
5. Estimated property value
6. Desired loan amount

**Changed Circumstances:** A revised LE must be issued within 3 business days if a "changed circumstance" causes increases in certain costs.

### Closing Disclosure (CD)
**Required by:** 12 CFR § 1026.19(f)
**When:** Must be received by the borrower at least 3 business days before loan consummation (closing).
**Purpose:** Shows actual loan terms, costs, and funds needed to close.

**Waiting Period:** The 3-business-day waiting period restarts if:
- APR increases by more than 0.125%
- Loan product changes (e.g., fixed to ARM)
- Prepayment penalty is added

### TRID Tolerance Categories
- **Zero Tolerance:** Origination charges, transfer taxes (cannot increase at all)
- **10% Tolerance:** Third-party services (can increase up to 10% in aggregate)
- **Unlimited Tolerance:** Prepaid items, homeowner's insurance, reserves

## ECOA — Equal Credit Opportunity Act (Regulation B)

**Regulation:** 12 CFR § 1002
**Enforced by:** CFPB, banking regulators

**Prohibited Bases:** Creditors may not discriminate based on:
- Race, color, religion, national origin
- Sex or marital status
- Age (provided the applicant has capacity to contract)
- Receipt of income from public assistance programs
- Exercise of any right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act

### Adverse Action Requirements
When a loan is denied, the creditor must:
1. Provide notice within 30 days of application
2. State specific reasons for denial (or inform borrower of right to request reasons)
3. Include the ECOA statement of rights
4. Provide credit score disclosure if a credit score was used (FCRA § 615)

**Statement of Credit Denial/Action:** Must include specific reasons — not just "your credit is insufficient."

### Appraisal Requirements (ECOA)
Creditors must provide applicants a free copy of any appraisal or other written valuation used in the loan decision promptly upon completion, or 3 business days before closing.

## HMDA — Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (Regulation C)

**Regulation:** 12 CFR § 1003
**Enforced by:** CFPB

Requires covered financial institutions to collect and report data about mortgage applications and loans. Purpose: identify possible discriminatory lending patterns and ensure lenders are serving their communities.

### Required Data Points
- Loan type, purpose, property type
- Loan amount
- Geographic information (census tract)
- Applicant demographic information (ethnicity, race, sex)
- Income
- Credit score range
- Rate spread (for higher-priced loans)
- AUS result
- Denial reasons

### Demographic Information Collection
Lenders must ask for race, ethnicity, and sex on mortgage applications. Applicants may decline to provide this information ("information not provided" is an acceptable response). Lenders must note visual observation if applicant declines to state.

## RESPA — Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (Regulation X)

**Regulation:** 12 CFR § 1024

Key provisions:
- Prohibits kickbacks between settlement service providers (Section 8)
- Regulates escrow account practices (Section 10)
- Requires disclosure of Affiliated Business Arrangements
- Enforces TRID through regulation X working with Regulation Z

**Escrow Account Limits:** Lenders may collect up to 2 months' property taxes and insurance as initial escrow cushion. Monthly escrow payment is limited to 1/12th of annual obligations plus 1/6th cushion.

## FCRA — Fair Credit Reporting Act

**U.S. Code:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681

Regulates the collection, dissemination, and use of consumer credit information.

**Key Mortgage Provisions:**
- Borrowers must consent to credit inquiries
- Hard inquiries appear on credit report for 2 years, affect score for ~12 months
- Rate-shopping mortgage inquiries within 14–45 days count as a single inquiry (credit score protection)
- Adverse action credit score disclosure: if credit score used, must provide score, range, date, bureau, and key factors

## GLBA — Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (Privacy of Financial Information)

**U.S. Code:** 15 U.S.C. § 6801

Requires financial institutions to:
- Provide privacy notices to customers
- Safeguard customer financial information
- Give customers right to opt out of sharing with non-affiliated third parties

Applicable to all mortgage lenders who collect personal financial information.

## E-SIGN Act / UETA

**Federal:** 15 U.S.C. § 7001 (E-SIGN Act)
**State:** Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA)

Enables mortgage documents and disclosures to be signed and delivered electronically.

**Requirements:**
1. Consumer must affirmatively consent to electronic delivery
2. Consent must be in a format the consumer can access
3. Consumer must be able to withdraw consent at any time
4. Creditor must confirm e-consent before delivering any electronic disclosures
5. System must maintain tamper-evident records

**TRID + E-SIGN:** Loan Estimates and Closing Disclosures may be delivered electronically after valid e-consent. Delivery is deemed received 3 business days after electronic transmission.

## Fair Housing Act

**U.S. Code:** 42 U.S.C. § 3605

Prohibits discrimination in residential real estate transactions (including mortgage lending) based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, or disability.

Redlining (refusing to lend in certain geographic areas based on race/national origin) is a Fair Housing Act violation.

---

## State Law Considerations

Individual states have their own mortgage lending laws that may be more restrictive than federal law:
- Some states have shorter adverse action notice windows
- Anti-predatory lending laws vary by state
- Remote Online Notarization (RON) is authorized in most states but not all
- State usury limits may cap interest rates for certain loan types

The platform applies the more restrictive of federal or applicable state law for each transaction.

|-------|-------------|-----------|

| Appraisal copy to borrower | 3 business days before closing (or upon completion if earlier) | ECOA |
