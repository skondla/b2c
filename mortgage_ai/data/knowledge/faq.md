# Mortgage FAQ — Common Borrower Questions

## Pre-Qualification & Application

### What is the difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval?
Pre-qualification is a quick estimate based on information you provide — income, debts, estimated credit score — without a credit check. It typically takes minutes and has no impact on your credit score.

Pre-approval is a more thorough review involving a hard credit pull and verification of income and assets. A pre-approval letter is stronger when making an offer on a home because it shows sellers you have been credit-checked.

On this platform: we start with pre-qualification (soft credit check, no score impact) and move to pre-approval during the full underwriting stage.

### How long does the mortgage application take?
The full application (URLA Form 1003) takes most borrowers 20–25 minutes. You can pause and resume at any time — your progress is saved automatically.

Pre-qualification results are returned in under 60 seconds.

### What is URLA / Form 1003?
The Uniform Residential Loan Application (URLA), also known as Form 1003, is the standard mortgage application form required by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. It collects your personal information, employment history, income, assets, liabilities, and property details.

### Will applying hurt my credit score?
The pre-qualification step uses a soft credit inquiry, which does not affect your score. The full application and underwriting require a hard credit inquiry (typically reduces score by 3–5 points). Multiple mortgage inquiries within 14–45 days count as a single inquiry for scoring purposes, so shopping around doesn't significantly hurt your score.

## Income & Employment

### What income counts for a mortgage?
Lenders look at qualifying income, which typically includes:
- W-2 employee wages and salaries
- Self-employment income (averaged over 2 years from tax returns)
- Social Security and pension income
- Rental income (75% of gross rental income)
- Alimony and child support (if consistently received)
- Investment income (averaged over 2 years)

Commission income must be received for at least 12 months and is typically averaged over 2 years.

### How much income do I need?
The key metric is your Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio:
- **Front-end DTI** (housing payment ÷ gross income): Most lenders prefer under 28–31%
- **Back-end DTI** (all debt payments ÷ gross income): Most loans allow 43–57% depending on loan type

Example: If your gross income is $8,000/month, a typical lender wants your total monthly debt payments (mortgage + car + student loans + credit cards) under $3,600 (45% DTI).

### I'm self-employed. Can I get a mortgage?
Yes. Self-employed borrowers typically need:
- 2 years of federal tax returns (personal and business)
- Year-to-date profit & loss statement
- Business bank statements (12–24 months)

Qualifying income is averaged from the 2 most recent tax returns. Write-offs that reduce taxable income also reduce qualifying income.

## Credit Score

### What credit score do I need?
- **Conventional loan:** 620 minimum (better rates at 740+)
- **FHA loan:** 580 with 3.5% down; 500–579 with 10% down
- **VA loan:** No official minimum, but most lenders require 580–620
- **Jumbo loan:** 700–720 minimum

### How can I improve my credit score quickly?
1. Pay all bills on time (biggest factor — 35% of FICO score)
2. Pay down credit card balances to below 30% of credit limit (utilization — 30% of score)
3. Don't open new credit accounts right before applying
4. Don't close old accounts (reduces average account age)
5. Check your credit report for errors and dispute any inaccuracies (free at AnnualCreditReport.com)

Rapid rescoring services can update your credit score in 3–5 business days after payoff.

## Down Payment

### How much do I need for a down payment?
| Loan Type | Minimum Down Payment |
|-----------|---------------------|
| VA Loan | 0% |
| USDA Loan | 0% |
| FHA Loan | 3.5% (FICO 580+) or 10% (FICO 500-579) |
| Conventional | 3% (first-time buyer programs) or 5% (standard) |
| Jumbo Loan | 20% |

A 20% down payment eliminates PMI on conventional loans, which can save $100–$300+/month.

### What are acceptable down payment sources?
- Personal savings and checking accounts
- Gift funds from family members (gift letter required)
- Retirement account withdrawals (10% penalty may apply)
- Proceeds from sale of other assets
- Down payment assistance programs (state and local grants)
- Seller concessions (limited by loan type)

### What are down payment assistance programs?
Many state and local governments offer grants or low-interest loans to help first-time buyers with down payments and closing costs. Ask your loan officer about programs available in your area. Income limits and purchase price limits apply.

## Closing Costs

### What are closing costs?
Closing costs are fees paid at closing to finalize your mortgage. They typically range from 2%–5% of the loan amount.

Common closing costs include:
- **Origination fee:** Lender's charge (often 0.5%–1%)
- **Appraisal:** $400–$700 for the property valuation
- **Title search & insurance:** Verifies and insures clear property title ($500–$2,000)
- **Credit report fee:** $25–$50
- **Recording fees:** Government charge to record the deed ($50–$250)
- **Prepaid interest:** Interest from closing date to end of month
- **Escrow setup:** Initial deposit for taxes and insurance (2–3 months)

### Can I roll closing costs into my mortgage?
For some loan types, yes. FHA upfront MIP (1.75%) can always be financed. Some conventional programs allow you to accept a slightly higher interest rate in exchange for a lender credit to cover closing costs ("no-closing-cost" mortgage). VA funding fee can be financed.

### What is a Loan Estimate?
The Loan Estimate (LE) is a federally required 3-page document you receive within 3 business days of applying. It shows:
- Loan terms (amount, rate, payment)
- Projected monthly payment
- Estimated closing costs
- Total interest paid over the loan

Review it carefully and compare it to your Closing Disclosure at closing.

## The Mortgage Process

### What happens after I submit my application?
1. **Processing:** Your loan officer and processor review your file and order the appraisal
2. **Appraisal:** An independent appraiser visits the property and provides a value opinion
3. **Underwriting:** An underwriter reviews all documents, runs your loan through AUS (automated underwriting), and issues a decision
4. **Conditional Approval:** Most loans get approved with conditions (documents to provide, explanations needed)
5. **Clear to Close (CTC):** All conditions are cleared, you're approved to close
6. **Closing Disclosure:** Sent at least 3 business days before closing
7. **Closing:** Sign documents, pay closing costs, get your keys

Timeline: 30–45 days is typical from application to closing. Our platform targets conditional approval in under 24 hours.

### What is automated underwriting (AUS)?
Automated Underwriting Systems (AUS) are computer programs that analyze your loan file and provide a credit risk recommendation. The two main systems are:
- **Desktop Underwriter (DU)** by Fannie Mae — for conventional and FHA loans
- **Loan Product Advisor (LPA)** by Freddie Mac — for conventional loans

AUS findings: "Approve/Eligible" (good to go), "Refer" (needs human underwriter review), "Refer with Caution" (significant concerns).

### What are underwriting conditions?
Conditions are items the underwriter needs before they can issue final approval. Common conditions:
- Letter of explanation (LOX) for large deposits or credit inquiries
- Additional bank statements or pay stubs
- Updated title commitment
- Homeowner's insurance binder
- HOA certification (for condos)

Respond to conditions quickly — the clock on your rate lock is running!

## Rate Lock

### What is a rate lock?
A rate lock is a lender commitment to hold a specific interest rate for a set period (usually 30, 45, or 60 days) while your loan is processed. It protects you from rate increases between application and closing.

### When should I lock my rate?
Lock when you're comfortable with the rate AND have a realistic closing timeline. Most borrowers lock at application or when their offer is accepted. A 30-day lock is standard; choose 45–60 days if you expect processing delays.

### What happens if my lock expires?
If closing takes longer than your lock period, you'll need a rate lock extension. Extensions typically cost 0.125%–0.25% of the loan amount per 30 days. If rates went down during your lock period, you cannot automatically get the lower rate unless you have a "float-down" option (available on some longer locks).

## After Closing

### When is my first payment due?
Mortgage payments are paid in arrears, so your first payment is due 1–2 months after closing. For example, if you close on March 15, your first payment is likely due May 1 (covering April's interest).

### Will my payment change?
For fixed-rate loans, your principal and interest (P&I) payment never changes. However, your total monthly payment (including escrow for taxes and insurance) may change annually when your lender recalculates your escrow.

### How do I build equity faster?
- Make extra principal payments (even $100/month makes a significant difference)
- Choose a 15-year term instead of 30-year
- Refinance to a lower rate if rates drop 1%+ below your current rate
- Property appreciation increases equity without any action from you

## Glossary

- **APR (Annual Percentage Rate):** The true cost of borrowing, including interest rate plus fees, expressed as a yearly rate
- **DTI (Debt-to-Income Ratio):** Monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income
- **LTV (Loan-to-Value Ratio):** Loan amount ÷ home value; lower LTV = better rates
- **PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance):** Required on conventional loans with < 20% down
- **MIP (Mortgage Insurance Premium):** FHA's equivalent of PMI; includes upfront and annual components
- **PITI (Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance):** Total monthly housing payment
- **Escrow:** Account held by lender to pay property taxes and insurance on your behalf
- **Title:** Legal ownership of a property
- **Amortization:** The process of paying off a loan through regular payments over time
- **Equity:** The difference between your home's value and what you owe on it
